## gharAmnAya Late survival?
Source: [TW](https://x.com/blog_supplement/status/2093913056099135736)

These are two intriguing fragmentary terracottas from kAnyakubja depicting skanda and ShaShThI. They were published as being medieval rather than gupta, as most of the famous terracottas from the city are. We are not entirely sure how they reached the conclusion, but it may be because they show less wear than the old gupta terracottas. 

If they are indeed medieval, then they would be fleeting evidence for the survival of the central kaumAra lodge (gharAmnAya) in medieval and subsequently modern times. A rare surviving exponent of the central lodge interacted with the eastern lodge in early modern times. 

## mahAsthAna
The eastern lodge stationed at mahAsthAna in the va~Nga country was destroyed when the va~Nga fell to the Mohammadans. However, survivors of it fled in at least two directions. The famous inscription from the kaumAra tapovana at Bellary, Karnataka, indicates that some ascetics from the eastern lodge moved to the karNATa country. 

Other members fled to Burma. Despite being bauddha, the Burman rAjan performed annual kaumAra rites for his military success. We have a record that king bodhawpAya celebrated an annual skanda-maha (kArttika shukla ShaShThI) down to 1811 CE. 

For that celebration, one of the rare surviving kaumAra-s of the central gharAmnAya named govinda-mahArAjendra Agama-dharma-rAja-guru from vArANasi conducted the elaborate kaumAra ritual in Burma, including revival rites at the two ancient skanda shrines in Pagan.
