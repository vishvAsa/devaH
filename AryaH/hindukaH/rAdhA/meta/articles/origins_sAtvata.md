## Exterior origin
The Pañcatantra "Viṣṇu as weaver" tale notably refers to Rādhā as the bhāryā (wife) of Viṣṇu, a rather perplexing statement given that "Rādhā" is not listed anywhere among the mahiṣīs of Kṛṣṇa.

Unlike Rukmiṇī, Jāmbavatī, Satyabhāmā, et al., she is entirely unknown to the Mahābhārata (including Harivaṃśa), Viṣṇupurāṇa, Bhāgavatapurāṇa, etc.

## viShAkhA
In my opinion, Rādhā likely originated as a minor Śākta goddess possibly associated with nakṣatra viśākhā (cf. "rādhe viśākhe suhavā 'nurādhā").

Neighboring nakShatras anurādhā and Viśākhā have been distinct since atleast 4k years, when the naxatra-sUkta of the taittirIyas was composed.

Rādhā and Viśākhā were initially interchangeable names for a single goddess yet became distinct gopīs by the 10th century AD at the latest  since Jayavallabha in his Vajjālagga clearly distinguishes Rādhikā (Rāhiā) from Viśākhikā (Visāhiā).


## lakShmI, viShNu association
This Goddess was increasingly identified with the Goddess Lakṣmī during the early centuries of the common era.

Due to her association with Lakṣmī and conflation with the word "rādhas" (desired object), this minor goddess was "+++(reverse-)+++euhemerized" as a "beloved" gopikā and the presiding goddess of Vṛndāvana (the locale of Kṛṣṇa's rāsalīlā).

The association with Viṣṇu was a secondary development as he is the "nakṣatrin," or "lord of the stars."

## Śākta ऒरिगिन्?
Śākta *texts* are generally late but Śākta praxis is generally much earlier than the texts. 
The absence of any reference to Rādhā in Viṣṇu Purāṇa suggests *against* Vaiṣṇava origin.

Certain Śākta traditions (e.g. identification of Rādhā with the Kāmeśvarī aspect of Tripurasundarī in the Kṛṣṇayāmala) are later developments, though it's clear that several "gopīs" such as Rādhā/Viśākhā (also known as Śūrpa), Anurādhā, and Dhaniṣṭhikā (also "Dhyānaniṣṭhikā") were never gopīs at all but rather independent goddesses who presided over asterisms.

## Pre-jayadeva
Given that Jayadeva references the other four pañcaratnas of Lakṣmaṇa Sena (Dhoyin, et al.), it's clear that the late 12th century is the terminus post quem for Jayadeva's Gītagovinda, with an early 13th century composition being more likely.

Śrīdharadāsa (a contemporary of the pañcaratnas) quotes late (~Pāla-era) poets like Vasukalpa, Abhinanda, et al. in his Saduktikarṇāmṛta. These authors were unknown to Rājaśekhara, Murāri, et al. and must accordingly postdate them.  

Given the above facts, how can anyone argue that Jayadeva "invented" the figure of Rādhā?

The presence of a fragmentary inscription from Maṇḍor (Maḍḍodara in Mārwār) which references Rādhā and paleographically dates to the late 8th (or early 9th) century is sufficient to refute such a claim.
