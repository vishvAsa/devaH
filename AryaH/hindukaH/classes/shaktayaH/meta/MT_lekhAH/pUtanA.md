In a similar vein, Pūtanikā/Pūtanā was originally an independent "apotropaic" folk-goddess venerated alongside Carakī, Vidārī, etc. who was "appropriated" (or maligned) as a demoness/ogress by Vaiṣṇavas.

Later Vaiṣṇavas even gave Pūtanā a "back-story" in which she was Bali's daughter Ratnamālā in a previous birth and sought to breastfeed Vāmana out of her maternal affection, yet her love transfomed into anger after Vāmana tricked and bound Bali.

While the Śākta Tantras which mention veneration of Pūtanā alongside Carakī, Vidārī, et al. tend to be (comparatively) late, the veneration of such deities is much older.
The propitiation of the Goddess Pūtanā is attested in the Uttaratantra of the Suśruta-saṃhitā
