Plant toxins as the poison of the Rudra-s (in _Maitrāyaṇīya Saṃhitā_ 1.8.4)

oṣadhīr vā imā rudrā viṣeṇāñjan | tāḥ paśavo nāliśanta | te devāḥ prajāpatim evopādhāvan | sa prajāpatir abravīt | vāryaṃ vṛṇai | bhāgo me .astv iti | vṛṇīṣveti | abruvant so.abravīt | mad devatyaiva samid asad iti | tasmāt prājāpatyā samit | deveṣu hy asyaiṣā vāryavṛtā | dve samidhau kārye | dve hy āhutī | ekaiva kāryā | eko hi prajāpatiḥ | ekadhā khalu vai samiddhaḥ | uta bahvīr āhutayo hūyante | tā agninānvavākarot | tā asvadayat | tāḥ punar navā ajāyanta | etarhi khalu vā agnihotriṇe darśa-pūrṇamāsine sarvā oṣadhayaḥ svadante | yat samidham ādadhāti sarvā evāsmā oṣadhīḥ svadhayāmakaḥ ||

Indeed, the Rudra-s anointed these plants with poison. The animals did not browse upon them. The gods ran to Prajāpati. He, Prajāpati, said: “Let me choose a reward; let there be a share for me.” “Choose!” [they replied]. He said: “Let the kindling stick (_samidh_) be offered to me as the deity.” Therefore, the kindling stick belongs to Prajāpati. For among the gods, this was chosen by him as his reward. Two kindling sticks should be offered, for there are two oblations. [Or] only one should be offered, for Prajāpati is one. Indeed, the fire is kindled in a single way, and yet many oblations are offered. He processed them \[the plants] with fire; he made them savory. They grew anew. Indeed, now, for he who performs the daily fire-offering (_agnihotra_) and the New and Full Moon sacrifices (_darśapūrṇamāsa_), all plants taste savory. Because he places the kindling stick on the fire, he makes all plants full of nourishment for him.
