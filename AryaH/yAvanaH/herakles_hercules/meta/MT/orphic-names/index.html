The Orphic Herakles

While the mythic Herakles of the classic Greek tradition is a typical Indo-European demi-god (ardha-deva) tending towards total Olympian godhood, the Herakles of the Orphic hymns is fully divine.
His cosmic nature is signaled by the appellation (see below, 34) that describes his 12 labors as being the passage of the sun through the 12 zodiacal constellations rather than being earthly actions.+++(4)+++
His primary hymn with the offering of frankincense presents him as a deity with characteristics of the Indo-European rudra class.

This nAmAvali-like hymn of 38 (the exact significance of this number is currently unclear to me) epithets has several interesting appellations that support this hypothesis.

  • Alkime Titan: Valiant Titan: This reveals his “asurian” nature, a feature shared with the rudra-class of deities.
  • Toxota, Manti: Archer and seer. As an archer, he overlaps with the domain of the original rudrian deity of the Greek world, Apollo.
  • The concluding incantation goes thus:

“Come, blessed, bringing all sicknesses’ charms,
drive out bad mischiefs with club in hand,
with fledged arrows drive out difficult dooms.”

These are classical appeals to a rudra-class deity.

  • As “khrónou páter”, he is in a sense “mahAkAla” = great-named Aion (epithet 32).

There are several more parallels, but we might return to them later. Finally, we believe that this rudrian nature (and duality -> mortal and god) of Herakles allowed his absorption into the shaiva tradition within the figure of lakulIsha.

The immediate question that arises is whether this was a grand culmination of the ongoing apotheosis of the epic hero, Herakles, or whether the earthly Herakles was a reverse-euhemerism of an already divine rudrian deity. In a similar vein, we can ask the same question of devakIputra vAsudeva and the pAncharAtrika vyUha-s as sAttvata heroes in the Hindu epic and the harivaMsha. Over the years, in both these cases, we conclude that there was an element of reverse-euhemerism of preexisting deities, colliding with the opposite tendency of conscious “avatarism” superimposed on earthly, historical heroes. The Orphic Herakles was relatively free of the later and presents his divine facet, much like the vAsudeva and the vyUha-s in a cosmic context in a pAncharAtrika text (both pre-tantric and tantric).

Appended below is the translation of the entire hymn from Malamis:

  1. Herakles, 2. mighty-heart, 3. great-strength, 4. stout Titan,
  2. strong-hand, 6. untamed, 7. brimming with mighty labors,
  3. variform, 10. time’s father, 11. eternal and kind,
  4. unspoken, 13. wild-heart, 14. much prayed-to, 15. omnipotent,
  5. almighty heart, 17. great strength, 18. archer, 19. seer,
  6. all-eating, 21. all-parent, 22. all-highest, 23. all’s help,
  7. who gave mortals respite, 25. banishing the savage tribes,
  8. yearning for youth-rearing, 27. bright-honored peace;
  9. self-grown, 29. untiring, 30. earth’s bravest shoot,
  10. flashing with first-born scales, 32. great-named Aion,
  11. who wears around his head dawn and black night,
  12. snaking through the twelve tasks, from east to the west;
  13. undying, 36. many-trialled, 37. boundless, 38. unshaken. Come, blessed, bringing all sicknesses’ charms, drive out bad mischiefs with club in hand, with fledged arrows drive out difficult dooms.
